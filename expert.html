Expert

An on-demand advice app that connects you with a verified professional for one specific question.

Project type
UX case study, concept
Year
2025
Role
UX research and design
Scope
Research to high-fidelity UI, six usability tests

The problem01

One specific question.

No good place to ask it.

When you need advice on a contract, a career move or a design problem, a course is too slow, AI is too generic and your network is hit-or-miss. Expert is a concept for booking a short session with a verified professional instead.

What changed my direction02

Two themes, not a feature list

I interviewed people about how they look for expert advice now, and clustered what they said. Two pain points kept coming back: knowing whether someone is the right fit before you book, and finding a moment that fits your schedule rather than theirs.

Those two themes decided what every screen had to do. Anything that didn’t build trust or remove scheduling friction became a nice-to-have.

Two ways people book03

Same problem, two speeds

The two personas from the research mattered most as two booking behaviours.

Quick decider

Needs just enough trust to decide fast, and will pay for confidence.

Careful comparer

Wants to compare options before committing to anyone.

Key decisions04

Built around trust and timing

  1. 01

    Put trust signals before the booking button

    Why
    People decide whether to book on the profile, not after.
    Evidence
    Theme one from the interviews. Later, in testing: “Diplomas and reviews make me trust it” and “Portfolios prove credibility instantly.”
    Changed
    Profiles show credentials, reviews and portfolio work above the fold, before any booking action.
  2. 02

    Show availability where people choose

    Why
    Clicking into every profile to check a time is exactly the friction the interviews described.
    Evidence
    Theme two. In testing: “Availability calendar is key, makes booking clear.”
    Changed
    Each expert card shows whether they’re available now; the profile ends in a calendar, and the booking flow starts from there.

Iteration06

Making availability legible at a glance

In the first version, “Not available” used the same orange as an available expert. At a glance the two read the same. In the revision the unavailable state drops back to grey, so only available experts draw the eye.

Before: same orangeAfter: grey, lower emphasis

Validation07

Six tests, and what came out of them

I tested the prototype with six participants across both behaviours. Booking held up. These were the problems that came up more than once:

  • 4 of 6didn’t understand some icons and found them by trial and error.
  • 2 of 6couldn’t find their way back to the Home / Dashboard.
  • 2 of 6found the Submit Review button less visible than Transcript and Notes.

One screen had both of the first two problems: after a session, the only way out was an unlabelled close icon. Below is the change. Labelling the remaining icons, making Submit Review the primary action and clarifying time zones are documented as next steps, not yet designed.

Iteration08

A clear way out after a session

Version A ended the session with a close icon in the corner. Version B replaces it with a labelled “Return to dashboard” button and brings the type in line with the rest of the app.

Before: icon onlyAfter: labelled button

Smaller refinements09

Two things I changed after feedback

Earlier
A logo on an empty screen while a session loads.
Problem
The wait before an expert joins is the most loaded moment in the app, and a static screen gives no sign that anything is happening.
Change
A progress indicator with short, supportive lines that change while you wait.
Earlier
A default system scrollbar in the transcript view.
Problem
Peer feedback: “I feel the layout looks a bit old-school compared to the rest of the app.”
Change
A slim scroll indicator in the app’s own colour.

Reflection10

What I’d do differently

Still open
Trust before someone even tries the app. The design builds trust from booking onward, but “should I try this at all?” is where most people would drop off. I never researched that moment.
Next
The documented next steps from testing: labels for the unclear icons, Submit Review as the primary action, and both time zones shown on bookings.
What it taught me
The most useful decisions in this project came from the affinity map, not from the pixels.
