Problem

Users struggled to find and book the right expert

Expert's platform offered access to thousands of coaches across various domains, but the existing interface made it difficult for users to discover relevant experts and understand their expertise. The booking flow was confusing, leading to high drop-off rates and frequent support requests.

01

Discovery was overwhelming

Users were presented with endless lists of experts without clear filtering or personalization, making it hard to find the right match.

02

Expertise was unclear

Expert profiles lacked context about their background, approach, and specialties, forcing users to read lengthy bios to understand fit.

03

Booking flow was complex

The multi-step booking process required too many decisions upfront, causing friction and abandonment.

Research

Understanding user needs and pain points

I conducted 12 user interviews with professionals who had used expert coaching platforms before. The goal was to understand their decision-making process when choosing a coach and identify friction points in the current experience.

Affinity mapping to synthesize research findings

Key Insights

💬

Trust is earned through transparency

Users wanted to see real session examples, credentials, and honest reviews before committing to a booking.

🎯

Context matters more than credentials

Users cared less about years of experience and more about whether the expert had solved similar problems before.

⚡

Commitment is a barrier

Many users wanted to "try before they buy" rather than committing to multi-session packages upfront.

Reflection

What I learned

Start with the problem, not the solution

Early in the project, I jumped to wireframes before fully understanding user motivations. Stepping back to conduct more research led to better insights and ultimately a stronger solution.

Test early, test often

Usability testing at the wireframe stage saved weeks of design time by revealing fundamental issues with information architecture before we invested in high-fidelity designs.

Balance business goals with user needs

The initial business request was to "increase conversions," but research showed users needed more transparency first. By solving for trust, we achieved both goals.
