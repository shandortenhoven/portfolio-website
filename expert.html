The brief

People don't always want a course. Sometimes they want a person, right now. Expert is a concept for an on-demand advice platform, connecting people with verified professionals for short, focused sessions. When you have a specific question (legal, design, career, health), online courses are too slow, AI is too generic, and your network is hit-or-miss. Expert tries to solve for the in-between.

This was a research-led project from the ground up. I wanted to design something that started from real user behaviour, not from a feature wishlist.

[ 02 ]

Interviews, affinity mapping, and the real friction

I ran user interviews to understand how people currently look for expert advice and where the gaps are. Clustering the findings into an affinity map surfaced two consistent pain points: trust before booking (how do I know this person is the right fit?) and availability that respects my schedule, not theirs.

Those two themes became the design's north stars. Every screen had to either build trust or remove friction from scheduling. Anything else was a nice-to-have.

[ 06 ]

Two interface details I'm proud of

The availability comparison screen lets users see expert availability side-by-side instead of clicking through each profile. A small UX gain that addresses one of the loudest research themes. The after-session screen closes the loop with a focused review prompt rather than a generic thank-you state.

I also iterated on loading states. Small thing, but it sets the tone for the platform. The wait between requesting and receiving advice is the most emotionally loaded moment in the flow.

[ 07 ]

Putting it in front of real people

I ran usability testing with six participants across both personas. The booking flow held up. The onboarding had two friction points I'd missed (both around expectations management) which I redesigned in the next pass.

[ 08 ]

The project where research-first became a habit

Expert was where research-first stopped being a slogan and became instinct. The most useful design decisions came from the affinity map, not from the pixels. That lesson landed for good here.

The thing I'd do differently is start the trust-building work earlier. Before booking, before someone has committed time or money. The current design handles trust well from booking onward, but the moment of “should I even try this?” is where most of the drop-off would happen in reality. That's a research question I never properly opened.
