[ About ]

A decade with people, now designing for them.

I'm a product designer in Amsterdam, with more than ten years of hospitality, leadership, and operations behind me. The detour wasn't a detour. It was the training.

01 / Path

From the front desk to the design file.

I spent most of my working life in the kind of roles where you sit across from people, listen carefully, and figure out how to make things work. Front of house. Reservations. Store management. Operations. The job kept changing, the underlying work didn't: understand what people actually need, build something that delivers it, lead a team through the change.

Along the way I picked up an analyst role too, looking at behavioural patterns and translating them into interventions. That was the moment design started looking less like a different career and more like the same job in a different medium.

In 2025 I started CareerFoundry's UX/UI program and completed it in 2026. The training gave me the vocabulary and the methods. The decade before gave me the instincts.

02 / Approach

Research first. Always.

My favourite part of the work is the early ambiguity, the moment before structure exists, where you have to listen and ask questions that feel obvious. I don't open Figma until I know what the actual problem is. That's not a discipline I picked up in design school. It's what years of customer-facing work made instinctive.

I'm comfortable with data and behavioural research as part of that early work. Typography, layout, and visual decisions follow from understanding, not the other way around.

The other thing the years before design taught me: how to lead a project through people. Stakeholder conversations, deadlines, handovers, the moments where things almost go wrong. I'd rather ship something considered and small than something big and vague.

03 / What I bring

The parts that don't fit on a CV.

Ten years of customer experience and service operations work. Comfort sitting with people whose needs are different from mine, and asking the right questions. Experience leading teams through process changes, including one situation where redesigning operational workflows brought staff turnover down by more than half.

I read situations quickly and I'm calm under pressure. I've held complex multi-channel service operations together with small teams, and I've translated user research into intervention strategies in regulated industries.

Also: a brief fashion design education at the start of the path, which is where I first learned that design is a practice, not a polish.

Design and prototyping in Figma. I write my own HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, which is what this site is built with. I work in GitHub for version control, VS Code as my editor, and I'm comfortable with Adobe Illustrator from earlier in my career.
