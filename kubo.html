Problem

Families struggle to stay organized across multiple tools

Modern families juggle countless responsibilities—school schedules, appointments, grocery lists, chores, and activities—often scattered across different apps, paper calendars, and message threads. This fragmentation leads to missed tasks, duplicate efforts, and constant stress.

01

Information is scattered

Families use 5+ different tools (calendars, notes, reminders, messaging apps) to coordinate, making it hard to see the full picture.

02

Mental load is unequal

One parent typically bears the burden of remembering everything, leading to burnout and relationship tension.

03

Kids feel excluded

Children want to contribute but lack visibility into household needs and expectations.

Research

Understanding family dynamics and pain points

I conducted in-depth interviews with 15 families (30 parents total) across different household structures—dual-income, single-parent, and blended families. I also spent time observing how families coordinate in real-time during busy morning and evening routines.

Key Insights

👥

Shared ownership reduces stress

When both parents have equal visibility and responsibility, the mental load feels more manageable.

🎯

Context matters more than reminders

Families don't just need alerts—they need to see why something matters and who it affects.

⚡

Flexibility beats rigid systems

Every family operates differently. A one-size-fits-all approach fails to accommodate diverse needs.

Solution

One unified hub for family coordination

Kubo consolidates calendar events, to-do lists, meal planning, and communication into a single, intuitive interface designed around how families actually work.

Key Features

Shared Family Calendar

A unified calendar that shows everyone's schedules, appointments, and events. \n Color-coded by family member with smart conflict detection.

Task Assignment & Tracking

Assign chores and responsibilities to specific family members with due dates. \n Kids can mark tasks complete and earn recognition.

Shared Shopping Lists

Collaborative lists that sync in real-time. Anyone can add items, and they're \n organized by category for efficient shopping.

Family Messaging

Contextual messaging tied to specific events or tasks, so conversations stay \n organized and relevant.

Building for consistency and warmth

I established a design system that balances professionalism with approachability— warm colors, friendly illustrations, and clear typography that works for both parents and children.

Reflection

What I learned

Design for diverse family structures

Early designs assumed traditional two-parent households. Research with single-parent and blended families revealed the need for more flexible role definitions and privacy controls.

Kids are users too

Initially focused solely on parents, but testing revealed children want agency and visibility. Adding kid-friendly features increased whole-family adoption.

Simple beats comprehensive

Early prototypes tried to include every possible family coordination feature. Scaling back to core essentials improved usability and reduced overwhelm.
