Kubo

A calm place for the documents that matter most when you need them quickly.

Project type
Mobile concept
Year
2025
Role
UX and UI design
Scope
Architecture, flows and mobile UI

The problem01

Needed rarely.

Needed urgently.

Passport, insurance, contracts, medical records: most people keep them in a mix of screenshots, email attachments and cloud folders. You rarely need them, but when you do, you need them fast, and that’s exactly when the mix fails.

The principle02

Complexity behind the scenes, simplicity in front

The sitemap looks busy because it describes the whole system: categories, metadata, reminders and settings. What the user sees is deliberately flat. Everything important is one or two taps from the home screen, and the rest stays in the background.

Never ask twice03

Three flows, one rule

Re-confirming, re-entering and re-authenticating break trust in a product like this. Every flow had to avoid asking for something it already knows.

  1. 01

    Adding a document

    Why
    Typing out document details is where people give up.
    Evidence
    The add flow scans, uploads or takes a photo, then reads it.
    Changed
    OCR fills in the details; you only check them and pick a category.
  2. 02

    Finding it again

    Why
    In the urgent moment you remember a word, not a folder.
    Evidence
    The retrieve flow starts from search: type “Passport”, open it.
    Changed
    Search on the home screen, with share and download right on the document.
  3. 03

    Before it expires

    Why
    The worst time to discover an expired passport is when you need it.
    Evidence
    The third flow is a reminder: Kubo notifies you, you renew or snooze.
    Changed
    “Expiring soon” sits at the top of the home screen.

Final interface04

A notebook, not a database

No nudges, no upsells. The visual language comes from notebooks and document folders rather than security software.

Reflection05

What I’d revisit

Still open
Sharing. Giving someone temporary access to a document is where most trust products fail. Kubo handles it at the end of the retrieve flow, but it deserves its own design.
Next
Treat sharing as the headline feature rather than an edge case.
What it taught me
Designing for trust is mostly restraint: the absence of friction is the reassurance.
