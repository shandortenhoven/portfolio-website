[ 01 ]

The brief

The documents of a life shouldn't live in screenshots and email attachments. Kubo is a concept for a personal digital vault. Passport, insurance, medical records, contracts, the kind of documents you need rarely but urgently. The current default for most people is a chaotic mix of cloud storage, photos folder, and the inbox. Kubo asks what a calmer, more trustworthy alternative could look like.

The brief was small in surface area but tricky in feel. This is the kind of product that has to communicate safety in every interaction. Any cleverness or friction breaks the trust it depends on.

[ 02 ]

A flat structure, not a deep one

Security products often hide behind layered settings and permission screens. Kubo goes the other way. A deliberately flat architecture where everything important is one or two taps from the home screen. The complexity sits in the background. The surface stays calm.

[ 03 ]

Designing for the principle of never asking twice

The core UX principle came from watching myself use existing tools. The worst feeling is re-authenticating, re-confirming, or being asked the same question I just answered. Every Kubo flow had to make that pattern impossible.

Never ask twice. Confidence and confirmation are not the same thing.

[ 04 ]

An interface that reads like a notebook, not a database

The home screen is intentionally quiet. No nudges, no notifications, no upsells. The visual language draws from notebooks and document folders rather than security software. Warm, considered, slightly tactile.

[ 05 ]

What this project taught me about restraint

Kubo was the first project where the hardest design problems weren't visible. They were about emotional tone. Designing for trust is mostly about restraint. The temptation is to add reassurance everywhere. The right answer is usually to do less, and to let the absence of friction be the reassurance.

The sharing flows are the part I'd revisit first. The moment when a Kubo user has to give someone else temporary access to a document is where most real-world trust products fail. The current design handles it, but the next version would treat that flow as the headline feature, not an edge case.
