MoodStream

Reimagining media discovery through emotion

Overview

MoodStream is a conceptual web platform that reimagines media discovery through emotion rather than traditional search or genre-based navigation. Instead of browsing by artist, title, or category, users explore music and film based on how they want to feel.

The platform introduces an immersive, spatial browsing experience where moods such as calm, euphoric, nostalgic, or intense guide content recommendations across both audio and video. The goal is to create a calmer and more intuitive way to discover media while encouraging exploration beyond algorithm-driven feeds.

Challenge

Most streaming platforms organize content through genres, algorithms, search queries, and popularity rankings. However, users often start with a feeling rather than a specific title.

"I want something calming while working."

"I feel nostalgic tonight."

"I want something intense and cinematic."

Existing platforms require users to translate emotions into genres or keywords, which creates friction in discovery. The challenge was to design a system that makes emotions navigable while still providing clear structure, usability, and scalable product architecture.

Key design questions:

  • How can abstract emotions be translated into intuitive navigation?
  • How can music and film coexist within one discovery experience?
  • How do we maintain clarity while creating an immersive interface?
  • How can experimental UI still feel like a real, usable product?

Process

1. Problem Exploration

I analyzed how current platforms structure media discovery and identified gaps in emotion-based navigation.

2. Mood Taxonomy Development

A mood classification model was created using two axes:

  • Emotional tone
  • Energy level

This allowed emotions to be structured into categories such as calm, nostalgic, intense, and euphoric, creating a scalable tagging system for media.

3. Concept Exploration

Three design directions were explored:

Cinematic Minimal
Premium streaming aesthetic

Playful Emotional
Color-coded mood interface

Experimental Immersive
Spatial mood exploration

The experimental immersive direction was chosen because it best supported the concept of emotional discovery.

4. System Design

A design system was created to support scalability, including:

  • Emotion-driven color tokens
  • Atmospheric background layers
  • Spatial layout principles
  • Reusable media components

5. Interface Design

Key product screens were designed including:

  • Landing experience
  • Mood selection interface
  • Discovery results
  • Media player
  • Personal mood library

Design Decisions

Emotion as Navigation

Rather than traditional categories, moods act as the primary entry point. Users explore emotions first and content second. This reframes discovery from searching for content to choosing an experience.

Spatial Mood Exploration

Instead of static menus, the interface presents moods within a spatial environment that encourages exploration and curiosity. This design supports the idea that emotional states are fluid rather than fixed categories.

Cross-Media Discovery

Music and film are integrated into a single discovery experience. This encourages users to move between media types naturally depending on their emotional context.

Atmospheric UI

The interface uses layered gradients, depth, and motion to create an immersive environment while maintaining strong hierarchy and readability. The goal was to balance visual immersion with usability.

Compassionate Insights

During exploration, several human-centered insights emerged:

People rarely search for media with perfect clarity
Users often begin with a vague emotional state rather than a specific title or artist.

Emotions are fluid
A user's mood can shift during a session, so discovery should feel exploratory rather than transactional.

Overly complex interfaces increase cognitive load
While the concept encourages immersion, clarity and simplicity remain essential for usability.

These insights guided the balance between experimental design and clear product structure.

Outcome

MoodStream demonstrates how emotional intent can reshape media discovery by replacing traditional search-driven interfaces with a more intuitive and immersive experience.

The final concept includes:

  • A scalable mood taxonomy system
  • An experimental spatial browsing interface
  • A reusable design system for immersive media products
  • Integrated discovery across music and film

The project highlights how product design can translate abstract human experiences into structured, usable digital systems.
